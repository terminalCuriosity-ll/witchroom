# Witchroom timeline

Status: **agreed 2026-10-19 (Steady pace)**. Numbers are estimates; we re-plan at each milestone.

## The pace
Built around a full-time job, so it's slow on purpose.

- **About 3 to 4 hours a week**: two short weekday sessions (45 min each) and one
  relaxed weekend session (about 2 hours). Skipping a week is fine and changes nothing
  but the end date.
- **Every 6th week is a rest week.** No building, just using the room and noting what
  you love or hate.
- **Every session ends on something that works**, saved to git. If a session runs out
  mid-problem, we stop at the last working version, never in a broken state.
- **No rebuilds on a work night after 22:00.** If something breaks, you want time and
  sleep to fix it calmly.

## The plan (in weeks of that pace)
| Step | What | Weeks | Roughly when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Ground: vibe, mood board, design language | 2 | Weeks 1-2 |
| 1 | Foundation: tiny config that boots your laptop, git basics, rollbacks | 3 | Weeks 3-5 |
| 2 | Walls: install and learn the window manager | 2 | Weeks 7-8 (after rest week 6) |
| 3 | Theme file: one place for every colour and font | 1 | Week 9 |
| 4 | Terminal and spellbook | 2 | Weeks 10-11 |
| | **Milestone: switch over.** The witchroom becomes your everyday system | | **~Month 3** |
| 5.1-5.3 | Clock, bar, workspaces (first real QML) | 6 | Weeks 13-18 |
| 5.4-5.6 | Weather, launcher, session menu | 7 | Weeks 20-26 |
| | **Milestone: a desktop that is fully yours** | | **~Month 6** |
| 5.7-5.8 | Notifications, control center (the biggest pieces) | 9 | Weeks 27-37 |
| 6 | Zen, file manager, lock screen, wallpaper | 6 | Weeks 38-44 |
| 7 | Ritual interactions (tarot draws, spells, gestures) | open | Month 11 onward |

Rest weeks fall at 6, 12, 19, 26, 32, 38 and so on.
**In total: a complete, cohesive room in about 10 to 11 months**, with rituals as the
open-ended fun part after that. The first time it feels like *yours* is around month 3.

## Why these numbers
- The early steps are short but new; most beginner time goes into "what does this word
  mean" and small typos, so they get extra room.
- Steps 5.1 to 5.3 are where programming starts. The clock is tiny on purpose: it teaches
  the pattern every later widget reuses, so later widgets go faster.
- Notifications and the control center talk to many system services (audio, network,
  bluetooth, power), so they are the slowest part.
- These are guesses from typical beginner pace, not measurements. We re-check them at
  each milestone and move the dates, not the pace.

## Check-ins
- **End of each step:** a short look back (what worked, what to change) and an update here.
- **At each milestone:** re-plan the rest of the timeline together.
